IELTS — Speaking

Practise out loud, against a real clock.

The Speaking test is 11 to 14 minutes in three parts. The single most useful thing you can do is speak for the full two minutes in Part 2 without stopping — so this page gives you the cue card, the preparation minute, and the long turn timed exactly as they are in the test.

1:00
Preparation time

Ideas to get you moving

    How to use this properly

    • Record yourself on your phone. Almost nobody hears their own hesitations until they play them back.
    • Take the full preparation minute and write key words only — never full sentences to read aloud.
    • Aim to still be talking when the two minutes end. Being stopped mid-sentence is a good sign.
    • Cover all four bullet points, but spend longest on the final "and explain why" — that is where the language gets more complex.
    • If you dry up, widen the topic: what it was like before, what someone else would say, what might change.

    Part 1 lasts four to five minutes on familiar topics. The examiner works through two or three topics, asking three or four questions each. Answer in two or three sentences — never one word, and never a two-minute speech.

    Part 3 is a discussion of more abstract questions linked to your Part 2 topic. Answers should be longer and more developed than Part 1 — make a claim, give a reason, then consider a limit or exception. Speculating and disagreeing politely both show range.

    These are functions, not scripts. Examiners recognise memorised chunks instantly, but knowing how to buy a second of thinking time, or how to concede a point gracefully, is genuine fluency.

    What you are actually marked on

    25% each

    Fluency & Coherence

    • Speaking at length without long pauses
    • Hesitating to find ideas, not to find words
    • Connecting ideas so they are easy to follow
    • Self-correcting without derailing the answer
    25% each

    Lexical Resource

    • Precise word choice, not just wide vocabulary
    • Natural collocation
    • Some idiomatic language, used correctly
    • Paraphrasing when the exact word won't come
    25% each

    Grammatical Range & Accuracy

    • A mix of simple and complex structures
    • Conditionals and a range of tenses
    • Errors that don't obscure meaning
    • Control under pressure in Part 3
    25% each

    Pronunciation

    • Being understood without effort from the listener
    • Word and sentence stress
    • Intonation that carries meaning
    • Accent itself is never penalised
